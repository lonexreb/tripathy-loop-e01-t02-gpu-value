# Findings report: an autonomous AI Scientist for GPU rental price forecasting

Data: `gpu-price-pack-v0.1.1-handover` (cutoff 2026-11-06). Generated by `lab/report.py` from the experiment ledger, the champion states, the lab notebook and the valuation model; every number below is recomputed by `reproduce.sh`.

## 1. Research question
Can a self-improving agent, confined to a locked evaluation harness, discover forecasting mechanisms that beat the starter kit's `drift` baseline on probabilistic (q10/q50/q90) forecasts of GPU rental prices at 1, 3 and 7 days, with calibrated 80% intervals, and does point-in-time web news (Exa) add skill beyond the pack's own tables?

## 3. Methodology and the anti-leakage architecture
- **Locked lab vs mutable organism.** `lab/` (config, sandbox, evaluator, controller) is hashed at start-up; any change during an agent step is reverted from git and logged as `LAB_TAMPER`. The agent edits only `workspace/` (forecaster, research playbook, append-only notebook).
- **Temporal partition.** 11 weekly Discovery origins (2026-07-15 to 2026-09-23), a 7-day buffer (the last Discovery target is 2026-09-30), 5 Holdout origins (2026-09-30, 2026-10-07, 2026-10-14, 2026-10-21, 2026-10-24; 2026-10-24 is the starter's demo cutoff), and the frozen origin 2026-11-06.
- **Point-in-time sandbox.** Every table is cut at the origin by its availability date (weekly aggregator medians and gpueconomy weekly prints only once their week has closed; unreleased hardware hidden); a runtime assertion rejects any row dated after the origin. A unit test shows the slice equals the official `build_series.py --cutoff` output value for value. The catalog given to the forecaster carries only static keys.
- **Scorer parity.** The evaluator reuses the starter's own `_common` helpers (validation, pinball, last-value baseline). Tests check it against `starter/scorer.py` in frozen and backtest modes, and check the seed forecaster against `baselines.py --method drift` row by row.
- **Information firewall.** Discovery returns full diagnostics (per-slice skill, breach rates, worst series); Holdout returns only pooled skill, median skill, coverage and skill by horizon, computed in a child process. Candidate code is AST-audited: numpy/pandas/stdlib only, no file or network I/O, no `open`/`eval`, no pandas `read_*`/`to_*`.
- **Four promotion gates on Holdout.** G1 pooled > max(0.200, champion + 0.003); G2 median >= champion - 0.005; G3 0.74 <= coverage <= 0.86; G4 every horizon >= -0.020. Pass: champion overwritten and workspace committed (`PROMOTED`, git tag). Fail: forecaster restored, playbook and notebook kept (`REVERTED` / `RUNTIME_ERROR` / `REJECTED_FIREWALL`).
- **Exa ablation.** `enable_exa=False` makes every Exa call raise `PermissionError`. In `PACK_PLUS_EXA` the forecaster gets an `external_signals` handle pinned to the origin (`endPublishedDate` = origin, hits without a date or dated after the origin dropped, responses cached in `outputs/exa_cache.jsonl` for offline replay); the agent's own research searches are limited to origins <= 2026-09-23.

## 5. What the scientist learned (mechanisms, evolved rules, negative results)

Mechanisms that survived the gates:
- **Where the loss lives.** About 96% of the pooled baseline loss comes from the 80 AWS spot series (USD 2-10 per GPU-hour, moving on more than 80% of days); the 13 daily-statistics series add about 4%. The pooled score is effectively an AWS spot forecasting problem.
- **Momentum in administered spot prices.** AWS smooths its spot price, so prices drift in multi-week trends: corr(past 7-day log change, next h-day change) is about 0.5 on Discovery. A pooled regression on the 1/3/7-day trailing changes roughly triples Discovery skill over drift (+0.44 vs +0.15).
- **Stickiness through local volatility (H1)** and **conformal calibration (H3).** Residuals scaled by the 14-day mean absolute 1-day change, with quantiles of the pooled standardised residuals, give near-nominal coverage (0.78 Discovery, 0.74 Holdout) and collapse the band on zones that have stopped moving.

Negative results (falsified on Discovery, kept in the notebook):
- **H2 cross-provider error correction hurts.** Adding a cross-AZ consensus term (log of the same instance's az_median minus the zone's price) cut AWS Discovery skill from +0.44 to +0.33. Pulling marketplace medians 25% toward the other collectors' same-family median cut non-AWS skill from +0.06 to -0.32. Prices in different venues are different instruments, consistent with the pack's do-not-splice rule.
- **Cross-sectionally pooled models for rate cards** (scale-standardised residuals with momentum, consensus and IQR features) did worse than per-series drift (non-AWS pooled -0.02 vs +0.06). Step-priced rate cards have too few moves to pool across.
- **Pinning q50 to the last value for sticky cards** had no measurable effect: drift's median is already zero there. **IQR width scaling** moved the non-AWS slice by under 0.01 (neutral).
- **Recent-window recalibration** of the residual quantiles (90/180 days) was unstable around the AWS archive hole (2026-03 to 2026-06) and lowered skill.

Current research playbook (`workspace/scientist_prompt.md`, evolved by the agent):

```markdown
# AI Scientist research playbook (mutable; rewritten by the agent each iteration)

## Mission
Beat the champion on the four Holdout gates (pooled skill, breadth, calibrated 80% band, no bad horizon)
with forecasts that would also be honest at the frozen 2026-11-06 origin.

## Where the loss lives (check before every hypothesis)
- The pooled score is scale-dependent; read `slices[].share_of_total_loss` and `worst_series` first.
- Fix the regime that carries the loss. A gain on a 1% slice cannot move the pooled skill.

## Method
1. Diagnose from Discovery only: per-slice skill, q10/q90 breach asymmetry, worst series.
2. State ONE falsifiable hypothesis with a mechanism (why prices behave that way) and a predicted sign.
3. Change one thing at a time so the ledger attributes the effect; keep everything else identical.
4. Record the result in the notebook, including negative results and their magnitude.

## Guardrails
- Point-in-time only: use rows dated on or before the origin; never hard-code dates or per-origin constants.
- Calibrate intervals from residuals of your own model (conformal-style), not from raw price changes.
- Keep the band inside 0.74..0.86 coverage: a sharper band that breaks Gate 3 is a failed experiment.
- Pool parameters across series where per-series histories are short (< 60 days).
- Code: numpy/pandas only, vectorised, < 20 s per origin; guard empty histories and NaNs; always return
  every target series; enforce q10 <= q50 <= q90 and positivity.
```

## 9. Limitations
- The scored set is the starter's headline tier at the 2026-10-24 as_of (as in the official backtest); its tier gates use data up to that date, a mild selection effect shared with the starter benchmark.
- Holdout is reused across iterations; the +0.003 minimum gain and the breadth gate limit, but do not remove, adaptive overfitting to five origins.
- Index prints carry the publishers' current revisions; Exa publication dates are metadata, and page contents may have been edited after publication.
- The valuation is a scenario DCF on posted prices, not transaction or resale prints (none are shippable in the pack).
